About

I’m interested in what happens between the tool and the person using it.

I’m an Information Technology & Administrative Management student at Central Washington University, currently building toward work in technical project coordination, systems or business analysis, technology and AI implementation, project operations, and process improvement.

My coursework currently emphasizes Project Management and Artificial Intelligence, but the thread connecting my work is broader: I’m interested in how technology, information, people, and organizational systems fit together in practice.

The technology matters. So do the handoffs, assumptions, workarounds, people, and information around it.
Professional narrative

Messy systems are usually where I get curious.

I tend to be drawn to information scattered across different places, processes that make sense internally but create friction for users, projects that need structure, or technology that technically works but does not quite fit the people using it.

I enjoy figuring out how the pieces connect and turning ambiguity into something more organized, usable, and human-centered. Sometimes that means project planning and coordination. Sometimes it means examining how information moves through an organization. Sometimes it means learning an unfamiliar tool well enough to use it for a real deliverable.

My professional experience crosses higher-education communications, technical support, administrative operations, multimedia production, web and information design, and project coordination. That mix has made me comfortable moving between technical detail and the organizational context around it.

I learn technology largely by exploring how it works, testing it, troubleshooting problems, and applying it. I am less interested in knowing a tool for its own sake than in understanding what it makes possible, what constraints it introduces, and whether it actually improves the work or experience around it.

Long term, I want to help organizations adopt and improve technology thoughtfully by connecting technical capability with practical organizational needs and the experiences of the people who actually use those systems.

How that shows up in practice

Three habits I’m developing deliberately

01 / INVESTIGATE

Question the first version of the problem.

I try to distinguish the visible symptom from the system around it: where information lives, who owns decisions, what constraints matter, and whose experience is creating the signal.

02 / STRUCTURE

Make ambiguity workable.

I use project structure, documentation, information architecture, and visual organization to make complicated work easier to understand, coordinate, review, and improve.

03 / TEST

Prefer evidence to assumptions.

I’m increasingly interested in defining what success would actually look like, what evidence would support it, and what would make me change my mind about a proposed solution.
